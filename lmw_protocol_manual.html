What Causes Disease

Four different practitioners, four different explanations for the same underlying question. None of these are medically established — they're each author's core theory, in their own words, condensed.

Robert Morse

The Three Root Causes

1. Inherent weakness — genetic "memory" from your parents determines which organs are constitutionally weaker and break down first.

2. Toxicity — mucus (especially from dairy), chemical residue, and heavy metals build up in tissue and block normal function. Morse frames colds, flu, and most GI issues as the body's own purging effort, not a distinct disease.

3. Overacidity (acidosis) — most modern diets (meat, grains, pasteurized dairy, eggs, cooked tomatoes) are acid-forming. Morse frames most "-itis" conditions (gastritis, colitis, cystitis, arthritis) as inflammatory responses to acidosis, not independent diseases. Correction = alkalize the body.

Bernard Jensen

The Neural-Arc Reflex

Because the embryonic gut tube and nervous system form together early in development, and internal organs bud outward from that same tube, each organ stays neurologically linked to the section of bowel it originated from. Toxic buildup in a bowel zone "reflexes" (via nerve pathways) to the organ that budded from it — causing symptoms in a body part that can be physically remote from the bowel. Practical implication: treat the bowel first, even for a problem that seems to be somewhere else entirely.

Dr. Sebi

Alkaline / "Electric Body" Theory

Disease is attributed to acidity and mucus buildup, which is said to let bacteria and viruses thrive. The corrective action is keeping the body alkaline through an approved list of plant foods and herbs — mucus is treated as the mechanism, acidity as the root condition.

Arnold Ehret

The Formula of Life: V = P − O

Vitality equals Power minus Obstruction. "Obstruction" is mucus and toxemia from wrong diet, blocking circulation and organ function. Ehret's single-disease theory: there is fundamentally only ONE disease — internal uncleanliness. Named conditions (influenza, pneumonia, nephritis) are reframed as the body's own elimination effort focused on a specific organ. When elimination is repeatedly suppressed rather than supported, the condition becomes "chronic."

Ehret's system predates modern physiology by roughly a century and is not supported by mainstream medicine. Presented here as historical context for why the later practitioners in this collection share overlapping language.

Daily Habits — Diet, Exercise, Breath, Sun

Robert Morse His "Nine Healthy Habits," in his own structure:

Exercise

Walking, swimming, rebounding, tai chi, stretching, yoga, or passive aerobic exercise — movement drives blood and lymph flow, especially in the lower body. If you're weak, build slowly, but increase activity every day.

Deep Breathing

Lie on your back. Place a hand on your abdomen just above the navel. Breathe in through the nose without moving your shoulders — the abdomen should rise first. Once the lower lobes are full, keep inhaling to fill the upper chest, raising the shoulders. Exhale gently through the mouth. Practiced regularly, this is meant to alkalize the body and increase cellular oxygen use.

Sun (Color Therapy)

Morse's explicit caution: "The sun is acidic in nature, and too much is just as bad as not enough." Balanced exposure, not maximal exposure, is the guidance — the more alkaline your body, the better you tolerate sun without burning.

Dry Skin Brushing

Long-handled vegetable-fiber brush (not nylon). Brush feet, legs, and hands first, moving toward the center of the body. Do before a morning shower.

Saunas / Steam Baths

Sweating is framed as a "third kidney" — a major daily elimination route. If no sauna access, a hot bath with mustard, cayenne, or other herbs added to the water is offered as a substitute.

Slant Boarding

Lying head-down at a 45° angle to improve cerebral circulation and lymphatic flow in the legs — countering the effects of gravity from a lifetime of being upright.

Foot & Hand Reflexology

Rubbing the soles of the feet and palms of the hands daily to break up acid-crystal buildup at nerve endings that Morse says "reflex" to related organs.

Meditation

30 minutes, same time daily if possible. Ten deep breaths to start, then progressive relaxation from the toes upward.

Liver & Gallbladder Flush

Robert Morse

Removes what the source calls "liver and gallbladder stones" — most are soft lipid/bile stones, not hard mineral stones.

What you need

  • 8 oz cold-pressed extra virgin olive oil
  • 6–8 oz fresh pink grapefruit juice, or juice of 2 lemons
  • Fresh apple juice (enzymes reduce nausea)
  • Optional: Phosfood Liquid to help soften stones
  • Optional: intestinal cleansing formula

Prep, 3 days before

  • Raw fruits/vegetables only (organic preferred)
  • 8oz fresh apple juice, morning and evening
  • Bowel detox: gentle formula if bowels move daily, moderate if every 2 days, strong if regularity is poor — or a single enema the day before instead
  • Optional: 45 drops Phosfood Liquid in apple juice, 2×/day
No solid food after noon on flush day — juice or distilled water only.

The flush itself

  1. Stop all fluids at 6:30pm
  2. Between 7–9pm: mix 8oz olive oil with 6–8oz grapefruit juice (or 2 lemons' juice)
  3. Drink it — all at once, or 1/4 cup every 15 minutes, whichever suits you
  4. Go to bed immediately after, lying on your right side

Watch stools the next day for stones — usually green, sometimes yellow/red/black, pea-sized to quarter-sized.

With any degenerative condition, this flush should be supervised by a health care professional.

Castor Oil Packs

Robert Morse

Used on the abdomen for detox; also for pain, slipped discs, tumors, tinnitus, nausea, inflammation, and swollen organs (spleen, liver, kidneys, lymph nodes, bowels). Especially used for lung issues like asthma.

What you need

  • Soft flannel cloth (cotton or wool), cut to size (10–12" for the abdomen)
  • Cold-pressed castor oil
  • Wax paper or plastic wrap
  • Heat source (hot water bottle preferred over electric)

Instructions

  1. Fold cloth to 2–4" thickness, saturate with castor oil
  2. Apply directly to skin over the target area
  3. Cover with wax paper/plastic wrap
  4. Apply heat over that (wrap in a towel if too hot)
  5. Leave on at least 1 hour — overnight if needed
Frequency: 3–7 times per week. The flannel can be reused — store in a glass container in the fridge.

The Cold Sheet Treatment

Robert Morse (attributed to Dr. John Christopher / Dr. Richard Schulze)
Morse himself does not recommend this in highly debilitated conditions, such as advanced cancer — the risk of over-exhausting someone with very little energy is too high. Requires an assistant.

Step 1 — Prep

A few days of raw food beforehand. Optional enema the night of treatment; optionally followed by a garlic implant (8–10 large garlic cloves blended with 50% apple cider vinegar / 50% distilled water, administered via rectal syringe) — described as very strong even for raw fooders.

Step 2 — The hot bath

Draw a bath as hot as tolerable. Add 1oz dry mustard powder in a cotton sack, 1oz ground ginger root in another. Optionally add cayenne pepper for intensity (try without pepper first). Coat sensitive areas (genitals, nipples) with petroleum jelly. Soak 10–15 minutes, drinking hot yarrow or ginger tea throughout to sustain sweating.

Have an all-white cotton sheet pre-frozen or chilled in ice water, ready for the moment you exit the tub.

Step 3 — The cold sheet

Submerge the whole body except the head. On exiting, your assistant wraps you immediately in the frozen sheet — the hot/cold contrast intensifies the drawing action on the skin.

Step 4 — Bed

Go straight to bed wrapped in the cold sheet, over waterproofed bedding, with a dry cotton sheet and blanket layered on top. Optional garlic paste on the soles of the feet. In the morning, shower and dry-brush the skin; examine the sheet for released toxins.

Essiac Tea — Formula & Dosage

Rene Caisse
This formula is historically promoted as a cancer remedy. There is no established clinical evidence it cures cancer — the source book itself only claims people have been "helped," not cured. Presented here as a traditional formula, never as cancer treatment.

The four herbs (dry mix ratio)

HerbAmount
Burdock root, cut6.5 cups (52 oz by measure)
Sheep sorrel, powder16 oz (by weight)
Slippery elm bark, powder4 oz (by weight)
Turkey rhubarb root, powder1 oz (by weight)

Preparation

  1. Mix dry ingredients; store unused portion in a cool, dark place
  2. Bring water to a rolling boil, lid on
  3. Stir in the dry mix, boil covered at reduced heat for 10 minutes
  4. Turn off heat, scrape pot sides, stir
  5. Sit covered, undisturbed, for 12 hours total (stir once at the 6-hour mark if you like)
  6. Reheat to near-boiling — do not boil
  7. Strain twice into a clean pot
  8. Bottle hot, into sterilized containers, immediately

Dosage

Standard: 2 oz, twice a day, 2 hours before or after eating. Ideally: morning on an empty stomach (wait 2 hours to eat), and at bedtime (2 hours after eating). Can be diluted with equal parts water.

As a preventative (lower dose): 4 tbsp (2 oz) at bedtime on an empty stomach.
If side effects occur (nausea, digestive discomfort, or a temporary increase in tumor size as the source describes it): stop for several days, then resume at half-ounce doses every other day, gradually increasing back up.

Enema Guide — 9 Types

Llaila Afrika

Water (distilled, spring, or boiled) or herbal tea injected into the rectum/colon to remove feces, worms, and impurities. A high enema uses a 24–32" colon tube. A colonic irrigation uses 10–30 gallons of water with an in-flow/out-flow tube setup via a proctor; without that equipment, the source suggests 10 gallons per high enema, done across 3 days instead of all at once. Tube and anus lightly oiled before inserting; water warm, not hot.

Emollient — softens hard, caked feces

Barley, linseed oil, prune juice, psyllium, castor oil, olive oil.

Nutritive

1–2 cups molasses + 2 cups horseradish + juice of 2 lemons, in 2 quarts of water.

Sedative

Cannabis sativa seed tea, valerian, or catnip.

Purgative

Liquid soap added to an herbal solution of ginger and horseradish.

Anthelmintic — for worms

Source notes worms feed on fermented/putrefied matter from constipation, meat, contaminated dairy, lunch meat, slow digestion, and stress. Can be felt or seen in stool, often crawl out at night due to blanket warmth, and can cause anal itching.

Salt water: dissolve 1–2 cups salt in warm water, add lemon, inject into rectum.

Coffee & Garlic — deep detox for severe conditions

Garlic acts as an antibiotic; caffeine stimulates colon muscle contraction to push waste out. Solution said to travel up to the gallbladder and stimulate it to release its contents.

Diarrhea

Herbs that are laxative and also remove worms: aloe, cascara sagrada, horseradish, senna. To remove worms specifically: asafetida, elecampane, garlic, quassia, wormwood.

Antispasmodic — for a colon that won't contract/relax properly

Chamomile, hops, lobelia, valerian.

Astringent — for a bleeding colon

Caused by straining from constipation, weak/prolapsed muscle, or poor diet. Ice-cold water enemas suggested, or: comfrey, wild alum root, lobelia, mullein, wild rue, white oak bark.

To reduce how often you need one at all

2 tbsp daily of flax seed meal, slippery elm powder, psyllium husk/seed powder, fenugreek, or chia seeds — natural fiber/mucilage, not a laxative, not habit-forming.

Checking your own transit time

Eat 5–6 tbsp charcoal powder or tablets, then watch for it in your stool. Food should spend about 4 hours in the stomach and 4 hours in the intestines — total transit under 16 hours. Longer than that suggests an enema plus dietary correction may help.
